\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]

\subsection{1. Core Objectives of the IMF}
The International Monetary Fund operates under six foundational statutory objectives:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Promote International Monetary Cooperation (PIMC):} Providing the primary institutional machinery for consultation, dialogue, and collaboration on international monetary problems.
    \item \textbf{Facilitate International Trade:} Promoting the expansion and balanced growth of international trade, contributing to high levels of employment and real income generation.
    \item \textbf{Promote Exchange Rate Stability:} Maintaining orderly exchange arrangements among member states and avoiding competitive exchange depreciation.
    \item \textbf{Establish Multilateral Payment Systems:} Assisting in establishing a multilateral system of payments for current transactions and eliminating foreign exchange restrictions that hamper world trade.
    \item \textbf{Provide Financial Assistance:} Making its general resources temporarily available under adequate safeguards to members facing balance of payments disequilibria.
    \item \textbf{Support Sustainable Economic Growth:} Helping countries design and implement resilient macroeconomic policies to foster prosperity and poverty reduction.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Balance of Payments (BOP) \& IMF Macroeconomic Functions}
\lecturedate{01/09/2026}

\begin{definition}{Balance of Payments (BOP)}{bop_def}
The \textbf{Balance of Payments (BOP)} is a systematic statistical record of all economic and financial transactions conducted between the \textbf{residents} of a country and the \textbf{rest of the world} during a specified time period (typically a quarter or a financial year).
\end{definition}

\subsection{1. Structure \& Components of BOP Accounts}
The Balance of Payments is structured into three primary interrelated accounts:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Current Account:} Records trade in goods and services, investment income, and unilateral transfers:
    \begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
        \item \textbf{Export and Import of Goods:} Visible / merchandise trade balance.
        \item \textbf{Export and Import of Services:} Invisible trade (software services, banking, shipping, tourism).
        \item \textbf{Primary Income (Investment Returns):} Cross-border investment returns (dividends, interest receipts/payments) and employee wages.
        \item \textbf{Current Transfers (Secondary Income):} Unrequited unilateral flows including personal remittances, grants, donations, and gifts.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Capital Account:} Records net international capital transfers and non-financial asset movements:
    \begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
        \item \textbf{Capital Transfers:} Debt forgiveness, sovereign grants, and migrant asset transfers.
        \item \textbf{Transfer of Fixed Asset Ownership:} Cross-border transfers of sovereign or institutional fixed assets.
        \item \textbf{Acquisition / Disposal of Non-Produced, Non-Financial Assets:} Rights to natural resources, patents, trademarks, brand leases, and copyrights.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Financial Account:} Records international transactions in financial assets and liabilities:
    \begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
        \item \textbf{Foreign Direct Investment (FDI):} Long-term controlling equity investments in foreign enterprises ($>10\%$ voting power).
        \item \textbf{Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI):} Cross-border investments in equity shares, corporate debt, and sovereign bonds for yield rather than managerial control.
        \item \textbf{Other Investments:} Commercial bank lending, official bilateral loans, and trade credits.
        \item \textbf{Changes in Foreign Exchange Reserves:} Official reserve asset transactions managed by the Central Bank to stabilize the currency.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{2. BOP Disequilibrium: Surplus vs. Deficit}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{BOP Surplus:} Occurs when autonomous international receipts exceed total autonomous payments:
    \begin{align}
        \text{Total Receipts} &> \text{Total Payments} \notag\\
        &\implies \text{Net Inflow of Foreign Currency / Reserve Accumulation}
    \end{align}
    \item \textbf{BOP Deficit:} Occurs when autonomous international payments exceed total receipts:
    \begin{align}
        \text{Total Payments} &> \text{Total Receipts} \notag\\
        &\implies \text{Net Outflow of Reserves / External Debt Expansion}
    \end{align}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{4. Major Core Functions of the IMF}
The International Monetary Fund executes its mandate through three fundamental pillars of macroeconomic intervention:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Surveillance (Economic Health Monitoring):}
    \begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
        \item Conducts bilateral Article IV consultations with member nations.
        \item Monitors global, regional, and national economic policies, exchange rate regimes, and systemic financial vulnerabilities.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Financial Assistance (Lending):}
    \begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
        \item Provides balance of payments crisis loans and precautionary credit lines to countries facing external liquidity shocks.
        \item Conditions loans on economic stabilization programs and structural policy reforms.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Capacity Development (Technical Assistance \& Training):}
    \begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
        \item Delivers specialized capacity building and advisory services to central banks, ministries of finance, and statistical agencies.
        \item Modernizes tax administration, monetary policy frameworks, banking supervision, and macroeconomic data systems.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
