\section{Determinants of FDI in Switzerland}

Direct investment in Switzerland is not driven by classical market-seeking motives, given Switzerland's small domestic consumer market ($\approx 9\mn$ residents). Instead, investment dynamics are governed by a tri-partite hierarchy of macroeconomic fundamentals, institutional governance, and sector-specific agglomeration economies.

\subsection{Macroeconomic, Institutional, and Sectoral Drivers}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Macroeconomic Stability:} Switzerland maintains low inflation (averaging $0.5\text{--}1.5\%$), insulating capital against real depreciation. While Swiss Franc appreciation deflates foreign currency asset values, it imposes continuous non-price quality discipline on Swiss industry.
    \item \textbf{Tax Federalism \& the 2020 STAF/TRAF Reform:} Swiss corporate taxation combines federal and cantonal rates, yielding competitive effective profit tax rates of $11.8\pct$ to $14\pct$ in cantons like Zug and Basel. The 2020 STAF/TRAF reform replaced ring-fenced regimes with OECD-compliant patent boxes and R\&D super-deductions (up to $150\%$).
    \item \textbf{Sectoral Agglomeration:} Strict patent enforcement, world-class technical institutes (ETH Zurich, EPFL), and specialized trade finance in Geneva provide irreplaceable industry ecosystems.
\end{itemize}
