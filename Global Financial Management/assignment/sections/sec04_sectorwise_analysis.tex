\section{Sector-wise FDI Analysis}

Decomposing direct investment capital stocks by economic activity uncovers a stark structural contrast: Switzerland functions predominantly as a specialized commercial, trading, and holding platform for inward investors, whereas Swiss outward direct investment maintains an exceptionally robust advanced manufacturing core.

\noindent\textbf{Key Comparative Insights:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=1.5pt]
    \item \textbf{Tertiary Dominance in Inward FDI ($77.6\pct$):} Inward capital is concentrated in \textbf{Wholesale Trade} ($\CHF\,235.2\bn$, $25.4\pct$)---anchored by commodity hubs in Geneva and Zug---and corporate holding entities ($47.9\pct$).
    \item \textbf{Manufacturing in Outward FDI ($37.9\pct$):} Swiss MNEs deploy $\CHF\,508.0\bn$ into foreign manufacturing, reflecting competitiveness in life sciences, food processing (Nestlé), and electrical automation (ABB).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Pharmaceutical and Chemical Sector Deep Dive}

The pharmaceutical, biotechnical, and specialty chemical cluster represents Switzerland's most dynamic direct investment vertical:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=1.5pt]
    \item \textbf{Inward Expansion:} Inward stock surged from $\CHF\,63.7\bn$ in 2015 to $\CHF\,109.9\bn$ in 2024 (\textbf{$+72.5\pct$ growth}), driven by foreign biopharma leaders establishing European clinical formulation hubs.
    \item \textbf{Outward Strength:} Swiss outward investment reached $\CHF\,190.2\bn$ in 2024 ($14.2\pct$ of total outward stock), driven by strategic cross-border acquisitions of foreign biotechnology startups.
\end{itemize}

\begin{mnecase}{The Basel Life-Sciences Triad: Roche and Novartis}{basel_cluster}
\textbf{Novartis AG} and \textbf{F. Hoffmann-La Roche AG} deploy tens of billions in global R\&D. By acquiring foreign oncology platforms (e.g., Genentech, AveXis), they secure foreign patent assets while anchoring fundamental chemical synthesis in Basel.
\end{mnecase}
