\section{Country-wise FDI Analysis}

Bilateral geographic breakdowns provide vital empirical clarity regarding geopolitical linkages and capital routing. A critical innovation in contemporary direct investment statistics is the distinction between the \textbf{Immediate Investor} (the geographic domicile of the entity directly holding the equity stake) and the \textbf{Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO)}, which identifies the country where the ultimate parent enterprise exercises controlling authority.

\begin{remarkbox}[The Conduit Phenomenon and Phantom FDI Discrepancies]
Comparing Table~\ref{tab:inward_immediate} against Table~\ref{tab:inward_ubo} reveals the classic \textbf{``Phantom FDI'' conduit effect} documented in international finance literature (Damgaard, Elkjaer, and Johannesen, 2019):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{The United States Discrepancy:} Under immediate accounting, the US accounts for $\CHF\,168.8\bn$ ($18.2\pct$). Under UBO accounting, US exposure surges to \textbf{$\CHF\,370.7\bn$ ($40.0\pct$)}---an increase of \textbf{$+\CHF\,201.9\bn$} ($+120\pct$). This proves that the United States is overwhelmingly the true dominant source of foreign capital in Switzerland.
    \item \textbf{The Netherlands \& Luxembourg Deflation:} Under immediate accounting, the Netherlands and Luxembourg combine for $\CHF\,384.9\bn$ ($41.6\pct$ of total inward stock). When traced to the ultimate beneficial owner, Dutch inward stock collapses by \textbf{$84.8\pct$} to merely $\CHF\,37.1\bn$, while Luxembourg virtually vanishes from the top tiers. US multinational corporations route their investments through Dutch and Luxembourgish Special Purpose Entities (SPEs) to optimize treaty withholding taxes prior to deployment into Swiss subsidiaries.
\end{itemize}
\end{remarkbox}

The United States represents the single largest destination for Swiss outward direct investment ($\CHF\,334.7\bn$, $25.0\pct$), absorbing more capital than any other individual sovereign state. Concurrently, Western Europe absorbs $53.0\pct$ ($\CHF\,710.9\bn$) of total Swiss foreign capital, illustrating Switzerland's profound economic interdependence with the surrounding European Union Single Market.
