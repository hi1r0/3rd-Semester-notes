\section{Trend Analysis: 2015--2024}

The decade from 2015 to 2024 witnessed historic structural shifts: the unpegging of the Franc in January 2015, the COVID-19 pandemic shock, the implementation of corporate tax reforms, and subsequent global monetary tightening.

\noindent\textbf{Key Empirical Observations:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=1.5pt]
    \item \textbf{Inward Contraction from 2017 Peak:} After peaking at $\CHF\,1,347.8\bn$ in 2017, inward FDI stock underwent a cumulative contraction of \textbf{$31.3\pct$} to $\CHF\,925.7\bn$ in 2024, reflecting BEPS compliance, corporate restructurings, and currency translation deflation.
    \item \textbf{Outward Resilience and Rebound:} Outward FDI peaked at $\CHF\,1,513.0\bn$ in 2020. Despite a mark-to-market correction in 2022, outward stock rebounded by $+2.5\pct$ in 2024 to $\CHF\,1,340.1\bn$, demonstrating persistent overseas commitment.
    \item \textbf{Expanding Net Surplus:} The net direct investment surplus widened from $+\CHF\,193.6\bn$ in 2015 to an all-time peak of \textbf{$+\CHF\,414.4\bn$ in 2024} ($+114\pct$), cementing Swiss net creditor strength.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{FDI Integration as Percentage of GDP}

With Switzerland's nominal GDP at approximately \textbf{$\CHF\,800\bn$} in 2024, direct investment intensity ratios reach:
\begin{equation}
    \Phi^{\text{Inward}} = \frac{\CHF\,925.7\bn}{\CHF\,800.0\bn} \approx \mathbf{115.7\%}, \qquad \Phi^{\text{Outward}} = \frac{\CHF\,1,340.1\bn}{\CHF\,800.0\bn} \approx \mathbf{167.5\%}
\end{equation}
These ratios vastly exceed average OECD benchmarks ($40\text{--}55\%$), confirming Switzerland's position among the world's most hyper-globalized economies.
