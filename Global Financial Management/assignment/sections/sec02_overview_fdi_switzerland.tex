\section{Overview of FDI in Switzerland}

A thorough empirical assessment requires distinguishing between \textbf{capital stocks} (cumulative valuation of foreign assets at year-end) and \textbf{capital transactions (flows)}, which record net cross-border financial movements during the calendar year.

\subsection{FDI Capital Stocks (2024 Year-End Position)}

According to official Swiss National Bank statistics, Switzerland maintained an inward FDI capital stock of \textbf{$\CHF\,925.7\bn$} and an outward FDI capital stock of \textbf{$\CHF\,1,340.1\bn$} in 2024 (Table~\ref{tab:fdi_overview_2024}).

Both inward and outward stocks are overwhelmingly dominated by \textbf{equity capital} ($95.8\pct$ and $97.2\pct$), demonstrating that Swiss MNEs and foreign subsidiaries rely on substantial core capitalization and retained earnings rather than volatile debt instruments.

\subsection{FDI Capital Transactions (Flows) in 2024}

In 2024, direct investment flows recorded net disinvestments across both directions: \textbf{$\CHF\,-83.4\bn$} for inward transactions and \textbf{$\CHF\,-11.1\bn$} for outward transactions.

\begin{remarkbox}[Deconstructing the 2024 Net Outflows]
The negative inward transaction flow ($\CHF\,-83.4\bn$) reflects three structural dynamics rather than a loss of underlying competitiveness: (1)~\textit{Monetary Normalization}: The SNB's exit from negative rates (from $-0.75\%$ to positive territory) and surging global yields prompted multinational corporate treasuries to relocate cash surpluses to high-yielding dollar markets; (2)~\textit{Earnings Repatriation}: US parent corporations repatriated European earnings to cover domestic US financing costs; and (3)~\textit{OECD BEPS Restructuring}: The introduction of Pillar Two minimum tax ($15\%$) catalyzed the consolidation and balance sheet downsizing of non-operational Swiss Special Purpose Entities (SPEs).
\end{remarkbox}

\begin{theorem}{Balance of Payments Stock-Flow Accumulation Identity}{stock_flow_accounting}
Direct investment capital stock evolves according to the dynamic balance of payments valuation identity:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Stock}_t = \text{Stock}_{t-1} + \text{Flow}_t + \Delta \text{Valuation}_t^{\text{FX}} + \Delta \text{Valuation}_t^{\text{Price}} + \text{Other Adjustments}_t
\end{equation}
where $\Delta \text{Valuation}_t^{\text{FX}}$ denotes currency translation adjustments (Franc appreciation deflates foreign currency asset values), and $\Delta \text{Valuation}_t^{\text{Price}}$ captures equity price revaluations.
\end{theorem}
