\section{Major Findings}

An empirical synthesis of the 2015--2024 Swiss National Bank datasets produces four core findings:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em, itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Inward Contraction vs. Outward Resilience:} Inward FDI stock contracted by $31.3\%$ from its 2017 peak ($\CHF\,1,347.8\bn$) to $\CHF\,925.7\bn$ in 2024 due to international tax harmonization and corporate consolidations. Conversely, outward FDI remained robust, expanding $+18.5\%$ across the decade to $\CHF\,1,340.1\bn$.
    \item \textbf{Expanding Net Creditor Surplus:} Switzerland's net direct investment surplus widened to an all-time peak of \textbf{$+\CHF\,414.4\bn$}, affirming its structural status as a global capital exporter.
    \item \textbf{Conduit Intermediation Unmasked:} While the Netherlands and Luxembourg appear as the top immediate sources ($41.6\%$ combined), Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO) tracing establishes that the \textbf{United States is the true dominant investor}, controlling $40.0\%$ ($\CHF\,370.7\bn$) of inward FDI.
    \item \textbf{Industrial Core vs. Services Skew:} Inward FDI is concentrated in services ($77.6\%$), while outward FDI maintains an advanced manufacturing base ($37.9\%$, or $\CHF\,508\bn$), led by the Basel life-sciences cluster.
\end{enumerate}
