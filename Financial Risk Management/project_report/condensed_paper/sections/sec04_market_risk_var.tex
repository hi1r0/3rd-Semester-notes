\section{Market Risk Measurement via Value at Risk (VaR)}
\label{sec:var_modeling}

Value at Risk ($\VaR$) measures the maximum expected portfolio loss over a designated holding period $T_h$ at confidence level $1 - \alpha$:
\begin{equation}
\VaR_{\alpha, T_h} = -\inf \left\{ \ell \in \mathbb{R} : \Prob(\Delta V \le \ell) > \alpha \right\}
\end{equation}
To quantify this exposure, we deploy two distinct methodologies:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em, noitemsep]
    \item \textbf{Parametric (Delta-Normal) Model:} Assumes Gaussian returns $R_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$, defining 1-day percentage and currency loss as:
    \begin{equation}
    \VaR_{\text{param}, 1-\alpha}^{1\text{d}} = V_0 \times \left(Z_{\alpha} \cdot \sigma_{\text{daily}} - \mu_{\text{daily}}\right)
    \end{equation}
    where $Z_{0.05} = 1.644853$ ($95\%$ confidence) and $Z_{0.01} = 2.326348$ ($99\%$ confidence). Scaling to the regulatory 10-day horizon follows the Square-Root of Time rule: $\VaR_{10\text{d}} = \VaR_{1\text{d}} \times \sqrt{10}$.
    \item \textbf{Historical Simulation Model:} Makes zero distributional assumptions, taking the empirical $\alpha$-quantile of sorted returns:
    \begin{equation}
    \VaR_{\text{hist}, 1-\alpha}^{1\text{d}} = - V_0 \cdot Q_{\alpha}\left(\{R_t\}_{t=1}^N\right)
    \end{equation}
    Expected Shortfall ($\CVaR$), which satisfies coherent risk properties, averages all returns falling beyond the $\VaR$ cutoff: $\CVaR_{\alpha}^{1\text{d}} = - V_0 \cdot \E[R_t \mid R_t \le Q_{\alpha}]$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{empiricalbox}{The Fat-Tail Wedge in Downside Risk Measurement}{fat_tail_wedge}
At the $95\%$ level, the parametric model slightly overstates loss due to central leptokurtic peakedness. However, at the extreme $99\%$ tail, the relationship inverts:
\begin{equation}
\VaR_{\text{hist}, 99\%}^{10\text{d}} = \text{\textbf{\rupee 61,487.02}} \quad \text{vs.} \quad \VaR_{\text{param}, 99\%}^{10\text{d}} = \text{\textbf{\rupee 55,048.61}}
\end{equation}
Historical simulation produces an unhedged 10-day downside risk estimate that is \textbf{\rupee 6,438.41 higher (+11.70\%)} than the Gaussian estimate. Furthermore, 10-day Expected Shortfall reaches \textbf{\rupee 95,130.94} ($19.35\%$ of total capital). Under the Basel Committee's regulatory Traffic Light System for 250-day testing ($99\%$), both models exhibit $\le 4$ exceptions and reside securely in the \textbf{Green Zone}.
\end{empiricalbox}
